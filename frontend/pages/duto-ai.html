Can you explain the difference between mitosis and meiosis?
Great question! Here's a clear breakdown:

Mitosis produces two identical diploid cells for growth and repair. It has one division cycle.

Meiosis produces four genetically unique haploid cells (gametes) for sexual reproduction. It has two division cycles and includes crossing over.

Key difference: Mitosis = identical copies. Meiosis = genetic diversity.
Thanks! Can you give me a quick study tip for memorizing the stages?
Absolutely! Use the mnemonic PMAT for both:

Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase

For meiosis, just remember it happens twice (Meiosis I and II). Draw the stages once from memory each day for a week — active recall beats re-reading every time.
